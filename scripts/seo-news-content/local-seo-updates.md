## Local search update log

Reverse chronological. Every entry carries a date, a source, and a label saying how well established it is.

<div class="callout"><strong>How to read the labels.</strong> <strong>Confirmed</strong> — Google announced or stated it. <strong>Observed</strong> — the SEO community documented it and Google has not commented. <strong>Independent testing</strong> — a third party published its own methodology and results. Nothing enters this log without a date and a named source.</div>

### October 8, 2026 — The September 2026 spam update has concluded

**Confirmed.** Google updated the Search Status Dashboard to confirm that the September 2026 spam update completed its rollout on October 8 after 14 days (it began September 24). It is the fourth confirmed spam update of 2026. Full coverage: [Google September 2026 Spam Update Concluded](/resources/news/google-september-2026-spam-update).

- **What changed:** Rollout complete after 14 days across all languages globally.
- **Who's affected:** Sites hit by spam policies, including local businesses using doorway pages, scaled thin content, or site reputation abuse.
- **What to do:** Check Search Console for manual actions and compare traffic before and after the rollout dates.
- **Source:** [Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/XhUDXP7A67iHCD2kmbVu).

### October 7, 2026 — Google local panels show a WhatsApp button

**Observed.** Search Engine Roundtable reported that Google is showing WhatsApp buttons in local panel results inside Google Search, sitting next to call, directions, website and share actions, and in some listings appearing in place of the chat button. It was spotted by Sachin Patel and replicated by Barry Schwartz. Google has not officially announced the change. Full coverage: [Google local panels show a WhatsApp button](/resources/news/google-local-panel-whatsapp-button).

Why it matters: The button draws on the WhatsApp number held on the Google Business Profile — a field Google began populating automatically for some businesses. While it does not alter rankings, an unmonitored WhatsApp number risks lost leads. Check your profile, confirm who responds, and set automated business hours in WhatsApp Business.

### September 30, 2026 — Google Maps asks for sign-in to read all reviews

**Observed.** Search Engine Roundtable reported that Google Maps now requires a signed-in Google account to see all the reviews on a Business Profile, and also to sort reviews or leave one. It was spotted by several people in the local SEO community. Google has not announced or confirmed it, and it is not clear whether this is a test or a full rollout. Full coverage: [Google Maps now asks users to sign in to read all reviews](/resources/news/google-maps-sign-in-to-read-reviews).

Why it matters: a signed-out visitor may no longer be able to scroll through every review or sort to the newest, so the reviews shown first carry more of the weight. Keep a steady flow of recent, genuine reviews, and put real testimonials and proof on your own website so your case does not depend on what the profile shows a signed-out user.

### September 24, 2026 — The September 2026 spam update begins rolling out

**Confirmed.** Google released the September 2026 spam update on its Search Status Dashboard on September 24, saying it applies globally and to all languages and may take up to two weeks to complete. As of October 8 the dashboard confirmed the rollout concluded. Search Engine Roundtable reported a second wave of ranking movement on September 30 — community observation, not a Google statement. It was the fourth confirmed spam update of 2026, and Google again published no list of targeted techniques. Full coverage: [September 2026 spam update concluded](/resources/news/google-september-2026-spam-update).

Why it matters: spam updates act on web search, so this window is relevant to your website's location and service pages rather than the map pack itself. If traffic to those pages moved from late September onward, check them against the spam policies local sites most often break — doorway city pages, scaled thin content, fake reviews — and judge the impact after the rollout finishes, not during it.

### September 23, 2026 — Owners get four days to reject a suggested edit

**Confirmed.** Google added a section on suggested-edit notifications to its Business Profile help documentation, dated by Search Engine Roundtable on September 23. Once notified by email or in-product, an owner has four days to accept or reject a suggested edit. If they don't respond, Google may publish the edit itself when other public information, such as the business website, supports it. Google also says some edits are applied without prior review. Full coverage: [Google gives you four days to reject a suggested edit](/resources/news/google-business-profile-four-days-suggested-edits).

Why it matters: a wrong phone number, category or set of hours can now go live because nobody answered a notification in time — and your website is what Google checks the edit against. Make sure profile notifications reach someone who reads them, and keep the name, address, phone and hours on your site identical to the profile. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/resources/google-business-profile-checklist) covers both.

### September 17, 2026 — Local knowledge panels are being rendered as AI Overviews

**Observed.** Barry Schwartz replicated the behaviour after Ben Fisher documented it: the local knowledge panel carries an "AI Overview" label with a "Show more" control that opens an AI Mode-style chat interface. There is no Google announcement and no confirmation that this is a full rollout rather than a test.

Why it matters: if this ships broadly, the first description a searcher reads about your business is generated from your profile, your reviews and your site — not from the copy you wrote. The practical response is unchanged and does not depend on the test sticking: make the underlying source material accurate and specific.

### September 4, 2026 — A fifth Business Profile verification method appears

**Observed.** Google began surfacing an option to verify by submitting a photo of the business location, alongside the existing phone or text, email, live video call and postcard methods.

### August 30, 2026 — Site reputation manual actions now behave differently inside the EEA

**Confirmed** — Google Search Central. Following discussion with the European Commission, from August 30 a manual action under the site reputation policy affects only the offending portion of the site for searchers outside the EEA, leaving the rest of the site untouched. For searchers inside the EEA the manual action's impact does not apply; instead the affected section may be separated in Google's systems so that it ranks independently from the rest of the site over time. Search Console notification, reconsideration requests and mediation are unchanged.

Why this sits on a local page: the site reputation policy governs third-party content published on a trusted host to borrow its reputation — which is structurally what a lot of local lead-generation sections and "best [service] in [city]" directory subfolders are.

### August 10, 2026 — Business name guidelines disallow repeated bilingual names and script transliterations

**Confirmed** guideline change. Repeating the same business name in two scripts or languages is no longer permitted — and Google states this explicitly applies **even when the name appears that way on physical storefront signage**. That removes the signage photo that owners in multilingual markets previously used to justify a dual-script name.

The examples Google used were Japanese, but the rule reads on any multi-script market — English alongside Arabic, Chinese, Cyrillic and so on.

### July 3, 2026 — Mass review removals hit Business Profiles, and Google called it a false positive

**Confirmed** statement. Profiles lost reviews at scale — some thousands inside 24 hours — and new reviews stopped appearing on affected listings. A Google spokesperson said: "When our systems detect suspicious reviews, we take a range of actions including removing reviews and temporarily pausing reviews on the profile to prevent further abuse," adding that Google was "investigating the issue and will restore any reviews that were incorrectly removed." The issue was subsequently reported resolved, with missing reviews restored.

Worth keeping in mind the next time reviews vanish: a spam-system false positive is a live possibility, and it is not the same diagnosis as an algorithm update.

### April 14, 2026 — Ask Maps behaves less like a listing engine and more like a recommender

**Independent testing**, not a Google announcement. Search Engine Land ran local service queries for plumbers, electricians and HVAC companies across five escalating levels of intent. As prompts became more conversational, Ask Maps returned a tight set of businesses — averaging between roughly three and five per query, generally in a three-to-eight range — and spent proportionally more of its output explaining *why* a business fits, framing them on qualities like responsiveness, specialisation and honesty.

Search Engine Land explicitly caveats the work: it draws on a limited set of queries in one geographic area and should be treated as "an early directional view, not a comprehensive representation across all markets or query types." We are repeating it here on those terms.

### December 15, 2025 — Google confirms Q&A is being replaced by Ask in Maps

**Confirmed** by a Google product specialist. Dan Boguslavsky wrote in the Business Profile Help forums that Google had "heard feedback that as our Q&A capability has grown over the years, it has become more difficult for customers to wade through all the questions," with customers instead asking questions directly in Maps and receiving AI-generated answers drawn from business information and relevant reviews. The rollout continued through 2026.

The practical consequence for local SEO: you can no longer seed your own questions and answers. The answer layer reads your profile, your reviews and your site, which makes those three the only lever you have over what it says.

## Quick answers

### Did Google confirm a local algorithm update in 2026?

No. Google announced no local-specific algorithm update in 2026. It confirmed core updates in March and May, spam updates in March, June, August and September, and a Discover core update in February. Local businesses were reassessed inside those broad updates, but there is no separate "local update" to attribute a ranking change to.

### What are the latest local SEO updates?

The most recent confirmed changes: on September 23, 2026 Google documented that Business Profile owners have four days to accept or reject a suggested edit before Google may publish it, and the September 2026 spam update, which began rolling out on September 24, has since concluded after a 14-day rollout. Google Maps has also been seen requiring a signed-in account to read all of a profile's reviews (observed September 30, not confirmed by Google). Earlier: repeated bilingual business names stopped being permitted on August 10, and a photo-based verification method appeared in early September. The dated record is above.

### Why did my map pack ranking drop when organic stayed flat?

Because they are different systems with different inputs. Map pack placement depends on your Business Profile, user proximity and Maps-specific signals; organic depends on your website. Before assuming an algorithm change, check whether the profile was suspended, edited, or caught by a review action — profile-level problems are frequently misattributed to updates. Our guide [Why did my Google Maps ranking drop?](/blog/google-maps-ranking-drop) walks through the checks in order.

### Is Google Business Profile completeness a ranking factor?

Google publishes no "completeness score" and has never confirmed one as a ranking factor. Claims citing a specific percentage trace to no primary source. Completing your profile properly is still worth doing, because an incomplete profile gives both users and Google less to match a query against — but treat that as sound practice, not documented policy.

### Can I still add Q&A to my Google Business Profile?

No. Google confirmed in December 2025 that Q&A is being replaced by Ask, an AI answer layer inside Maps, and the rollout continued through 2026. You can no longer seed your own questions and answers. Ask draws on your profile, your reviews and your website instead.

## Why this page is careful about what it claims

Local SEO has a sourcing problem. Google does not publish local-specific algorithm announcements the way it does core and spam updates, so the gap fills with confident claims — internal "completeness scores", exact proximity radii, precise review-velocity thresholds — that trace to no primary source.

We separate this page into two parts: what the confirmed 2026 record supports, and what we recommend from our own client work, clearly labelled as our practice rather than as Google policy. If you have been sold a local strategy built on a specific number nobody can source, that distinction is worth the read.

## What the confirmed 2026 updates actually establish

### Local businesses were inside every broad update this year

The [March and May 2026 core updates](/resources/news/google-algorithm-updates) were broad — they reassess how well content serves queries across all site types, local businesses included. Google's standing guidance applies unchanged: there is no specific fix for a core update, and the largest recoveries tend to arrive with the next core update rather than between them.

### Four spam updates raise the stakes on local spam tactics

Google confirmed spam updates in March, June, August and September 2026 — a higher cadence than the one or two per year that had become normal. The September update concluded on October 8 after a 14-day rollout. Google published no list of targeted techniques for any of them.

This matters disproportionately in local because the classic local spam tactics are exactly what the <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">spam policies</a> describe: keyword-stuffed business names, fake or gated reviews, lead-generation sites posing as local providers, doorway pages spun up per city with no real differentiation. If a local site lost visibility around one of those four windows, the first question is which policy it is violating — not how to improve content generally.

### Discover now favours locally relevant content explicitly

February's Discover core update stated an aim of showing users more locally relevant content from websites based in their own country. For local publishers and service businesses that produce local content, Discover is now a surface to report on separately from Search.

## What we recommend — and why, stated as our own practice

None of the following is a documented Google ranking factor. It is what we do for local clients because it is consistent with Google's published guidance and with what we observe. Treat it accordingly.

- **Complete the Google Business Profile properly.** Categories, services, service areas, hours, attributes, real photos. Not because a leaked "completeness score" exists, but because incomplete profiles give both users and Google less to match a query against.
- **Earn reviews continuously and legitimately, and reply to them.** Review gating — soliciting only from happy customers — violates Google's policies regardless of any ranking effect. Steady, genuine review flow is both safer and more durable than campaign spikes.
- **Build genuinely differentiated location pages.** Real service detail, real local specifics, real proof. A template with the city name swapped is a doorway page under Google's own definition, and there have been four spam updates this year.
- **Keep the profile actively maintained.** Fresh photos, current hours, accurate services — and answer suggested-edit notifications within the four-day window. Cheap to do, and it removes a category of avoidable problem. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/resources/google-business-profile-checklist) walks through it.
- **Get NAP consistency right** across the site, the profile and major directories. Unglamorous and still worth doing.

<div class="callout"><strong>On proximity:</strong> user location has always heavily influenced local results, and claims that 2026 specifically "tightened the proximity dial" by some measurable amount are not sourced to Google. The sound response is unchanged and does not depend on the claim being true: if you need visibility beyond your immediate radius, earn it with genuinely useful organic location pages rather than expecting the map pack to carry you there.</div>

### What this means for law firms

Legal practices face specific exposure under the changes documented above:

- **The four-day suggested-edit window requires designated monitoring.** Law firm profiles are frequent targets for competitor edits — particularly suggested category changes (e.g., from "Personal Injury Attorney" to a broader or incorrect category), altered intake phone numbers, or changed office hours. If firm staff do not review Google's notifications within four days, Google can publish those suggestions automatically. A single designated administrator must monitor notifications daily.
- **Review sign-in shifts weight to visible top testimonials.** With signed-out users on Google Maps restricted from scrolling all reviews or filtering to newest, the first few reviews Google displays bear the brunt of client first impressions. Law firms should ensure steady, legitimate review acquisition and showcase verifiable client testimonials directly on relevant practice area pages.
- **City and practice area doorway pages are at heightened risk.** Law firms commonly generate near-duplicate location pages for adjacent municipalities (e.g., "Car Accident Lawyer in City A", "Car Accident Lawyer in City B"). With four spam updates concluded in 2026 targeting scaled thin content and doorway patterns, templated city landing pages without genuine local case results, office presence, or jurisdiction-specific legal substance carry substantial organic risk.

## What to do if local visibility dropped in 2026

The short version is below. For the full diagnosis — suspensions, edits, reviews, competitors and the website — see [Why did my Google Maps ranking drop? How to check and fix it](/blog/google-maps-ranking-drop).

1. **Date the drop** and compare it against the [confirmed update timeline](/resources/news/google-algorithm-updates). Local rankings move constantly; only some of that is algorithmic.
2. **Separate map pack from organic.** They are different systems with different diagnostics, and a blended report hides which one moved.
3. **Check for a profile suspension or edit** before assuming an algorithm change. Profile-level problems are common and are frequently misattributed to updates.
4. **If the timing lines up with a spam update**, audit against the spam policies specifically — business name, reviews, location pages.
5. **If it lines up with a core update**, the work is whether your pages are genuinely the most useful result for the queries they target.
